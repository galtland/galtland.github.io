# Secession

> Secession is the withdrawal of a political unit from a larger one. Its libertarian interest is not any particular breakaway but the *principle* the act exposes: if a people may justly leave a state, the same logic licenses a region to leave that people, a town to leave the region, and — at the limit — an individual to leave the town. Secession is exit made collective, and exit is what forces a state to earn the consent it claims.

## The right to secede

The libertarian case for secession begins with consent. A government legitimate only by the consent of the governed cannot claim consent from a population that would leave if it could; the freedom to secede is simply the freedom to withhold consent made real. Once granted, the principle does not stop at any convenient level. If the United States could justly separate from Britain, a state could separate from the union, a county from the state, and so on down — there is no principled place to halt the regress short of the individual, who is the only unit that can actually give or refuse consent. Most traditions flinch before that conclusion; the anarchist wing of the libertarian family, in [[market-anarchism-and-private-law|market anarchism]], embraces it — individual secession is just another name for the sovereignty of the person.

## Exit over voice

Secession belongs to the family of **exit** rather than **voice**. Where democratic reform asks the citizen to change the state from within by voting and arguing, exit lets him withdraw from its authority altogether — and the mere credible threat of exit disciplines rulers who would otherwise face no competition. This is the mechanism of [[jurisdictional-competition|jurisdictional competition]]: a world of many small, exit-able jurisdictions forces each to treat its residents well or lose them, exactly as firms in a market must serve customers who can shop elsewhere. Secession multiplies the number of jurisdictions and so sharpens the competition; consolidation into ever-larger units dulls it by leaving nowhere to go.

## Hoppe's small-states argument

[[hans-hermann-hoppe|Hoppe]] makes secession central to the practical program of [[democracy-the-god-that-failed|Democracy: The God That Failed]]. His argument is that small states are *forced* to be liberal: because capital and productive people can readily leave a small territory, its government cannot tax and regulate heavily without emptying its own tax base, whereas a large or global state faces no such flight and can predate freely. Radical decentralization by secession — the multiplication of small units — is therefore, on his account, the realistic path from mass democracy toward a natural order of competing private-law jurisdictions, without requiring a revolution.

## The modern, non-territorial forms

The classical image of secession is territorial: a province raises a new flag. The wiki's cypherpunk and information-age threads describe a subtler version in which exit goes partly *virtual*. [[jurisdictional-competition|Jurisdictional competition]] and the [[the-cybereconomy|cybereconomy]] let capital and work relocate across borders without anyone physically moving; [[seasteading-and-network-states|seasteading and network states]] propose new polities on the ocean or online rather than by carving up existing land; and the [[the-sovereign-individual|Sovereign Individual]] thesis treats individual jurisdictional arbitrage — choosing where to bank, reside, and be taxed — as a kind of one-person secession the information age makes cheap.

## Where it is contested

Secession carries historical baggage: in the United States it is bound to the Confederacy and the defense of slavery, and elsewhere to ethnic partition and its violence. The libertarian reply is that the *principle* is neutral on what one secedes toward — a right to leave is not an endorsement of the seceding party's aims, and the same principle that would have let a slave secede from a master is the one the Confederacy abused. The sharper objection is the regress itself: a right of secession with no natural stopping point seems to dissolve every political unit into individuals, which critics read as a reductio. Libertarians tend to read it instead as the honest conclusion — that legitimate association is voluntary all the way down. The confidence here is uneven: the exit-disciplines-states logic is strong, while the claim that unlimited secession is workable at scale is genuinely open.

## Place in This Wiki

Secession is the political-institutional face of the wiki's recurring theme that *exit beats voice*. It connects the abstract case for [[market-anarchism-and-private-law|competing private-law jurisdictions]] to concrete proposals for [[seasteading-and-network-states|new polities]], and it is the mechanism by which [[jurisdictional-competition|jurisdictional competition]] is supposed to grow. It sits beside [[minarchism|minarchism]] as one of the wiki's answers to the question of how liberty is institutionally secured — not by capturing the state but by making it leaveable.
