# The Libertarian Family: Anarcho-Capitalist, Minarchist, Mutualist, Georgist

> The libertarian family is united by a suspicion of state power and a commitment to individual liberty, but it splits along a line most outsiders miss. The loud question — *how much state?* — divides the minarchist from the three anarchist positions. The deeper question — *what counts as legitimate property, especially in land?* — divides the Lockean camp (anarcho-capitalist and minarchist) from the mutualist and the Georgist. This page sets the four side by side.

| | **Anarcho-capitalist** | **Minarchist** | **Mutualist** | **Georgist** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The state** | None — polycentric private law and competing defense agencies | Minimal *night-watchman*: defense, police, courts, nothing more | None — voluntary federations of associations | A state, but ideally funded only by the land-value tax |
| **Property in land** | Full private ownership by homesteading and trade | Full private ownership | *Occupancy and use* — no absentee landlordism, no ground rent | Private *use*, but the rental value of land belongs to the community |
| **Property in capital** | Full private ownership | Full private ownership | Markets, but worker and cooperative ownership favored over wage-labor | Full private ownership — only land is treated as special |
| **Money & credit** | Free-market money and free banking (gold, Bitcoin) | Free-market or lightly regulated money | Mutual banking / free credit to drive interest toward zero | Orthodox; the reform is fiscal, not monetary |
| **Law & dispute resolution** | Competing private courts and arbitration | State monopoly on law and defense (minimal) | Voluntary arbitration and association | State courts |
| **Redistribution** | None | None | Via free credit and use-rights, not the state | A citizen's dividend funded by land rent |
| **Key figures** | Rothbard, D. Friedman, Hoppe | Nozick, Rand, the classical liberals | Proudhon, Tucker, Carson | Henry George |

## They mostly agree about the state

Three of these four want no state at all, and the fourth wants a very small one, so the popular image of libertarians arguing endlessly about *how much government* describes only the anarchist–minarchist border. [[minarchism|Minarchism]] holds that a single monopoly protector confined to defense, police, and courts is both necessary and sufficient — enough to secure rights, little enough to stay legitimate. [[market-anarchism-and-private-law|Anarcho-capitalism]] answers that even that minimal monopoly is an unjustified aggression, and that protection and adjudication can be supplied competitively like any other service. Mutualism reaches statelessness from the left, through federations of workers and communes rather than firms. Only the Georgist keeps a state — and even then, one starved of every revenue source but ground rent.

## The real fault line is property

What actually separates the family is the theory of property, and land is where it bites. The **Lockean** camp — anarcho-capitalist and minarchist alike — treats land as ordinary property: mix your labor with an unowned resource, and it is yours to keep, rent, sell, or leave idle, grounded in [[nonaggression-and-property-rights|nonaggression and property rights]]. The other two reject exactly this.

[[mutualism|Mutualism]] replaces title-by-trade with **occupancy and use**: you hold land and tools only while you are actually using them, so absentee landlordism and the ground rent it collects disappear, and — paired with mutual free credit meant to push interest toward zero — so does the return to capital the mutualist reads as exploitation. It is a genuinely market-based anarchism that nonetheless denies the ancap's core property premise.

[[georgism|Georgism]] accepts private markets in labor and capital but singles out land: because no one produced the land, its scarcity value (economic rent) is unearned, and belongs to the whole community — collected through a land-value tax and often returned as a citizen's dividend. Georgists are otherwise close to the classical liberals, which is why the position sits at the family's edge: fully pro-market in everything but the one factor it thinks the market cannot justly privatize.

## Where the wiki stands

The wiki argues from the [[market-anarchism-and-private-law|anarcho-capitalist]] corner — the Rothbardian line running through [[murray-n-rothbard|Rothbard]], [[david-friedman|David Friedman]]'s consequentialist variant, and [[hans-hermann-hoppe|Hoppe]]'s property-theoretic one. It treats [[minarchism|minarchism]] (including the [[objectivism|Objectivist]] and [[robert-nozick|Nozick]] versions) as the serious in-family rival on the question of the minimal state, and holds mutualism and Georgism at a respectful distance as market-friendly traditions that part company on the property premise the rest of the case is built on. All four belong under the broad tent of [[libertarianism|Libertarianism]].
