# Oppenheimer On Conquest

Oppenheimer on conquest names the claim that the state originates when a victorious group stabilizes domination over a defeated group. The point is not that every form of leadership is a state, but that the class state begins when force becomes a permanent institution of extraction.

> The State may be defined as an organization of _one class_ dominating over the other classes. Such a class organization can come about in one way only, namely, through conquest and the subjection of ethnic groups by the dominating group.

That sentence is the hinge between Oppenheimer's sociology and the later libertarian critique. In [[oppenheimer-the-state|The State]], conquest is not an embarrassing prehistory later redeemed by constitutions. It is the origin of the political form itself: a conquered population is left alive, settled, and productive because recurring tribute is more valuable than one-time plunder.

This is why the concept belongs beside [[political-means-and-economic-means|Political Means and Economic Means]]. The conqueror does not acquire wealth by production or voluntary exchange; he acquires it by command. Administration, law, and fiscal regularity then refine the conquest rather than changing its moral species. [[evolution-of-the-state|Evolution of the State]] follows that refinement across stages.

The qualification matters. Oppenheimer was not a Rothbardian, and his reform politics should not be imported wholesale. But [[our-enemy-the-state|Nock]] and [[anatomy-of-the-state|Rothbard]] could use his account because it breaks the contractarian myth at the root. The state is not society's chosen agent that sometimes errs. It is conquest made durable, legalized, and respectable.
