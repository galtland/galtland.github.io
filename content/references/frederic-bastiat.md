# Frédéric Bastiat

> Frédéric Bastiat (1801–1850) enters this wiki as the great popularizer of the free-market case: the originator of [[the-seen-and-the-unseen|the seen-and-the-unseen]] and the broken-window fallacy, and the author of [[the-law|The Law]]'s account of legal plunder.

## Place in This Wiki

Bastiat was a French economist, essayist, and member of the Chamber of Deputies in the last years of his short life, writing at speed as tuberculosis closed in. He belonged to the French Liberal School, and his genius was less for system-building than for exposing economic fallacy with a clarity — and a wit — that has kept his pamphlets in print for over a century. He argued for free trade against the protectionists of his day with a gift for turning their own logic against them, showing essay by essay the hidden costs their visible favors concealed.

The wiki draws on two strands of his work. From [[the-seen-and-the-unseen|That Which Is Seen, and That Which Is Not Seen]] comes the principle that sound economics reckons with unseen, foregone alternatives and not merely visible effects — the analytic core of the case against make-work, protectionism, and "destruction creates jobs" arguments, and the seed of Henry Hazlitt's *Economics in One Lesson*. From [[the-law|The Law]] comes his theory of legal plunder: the perversion of law from the protection of person and property into an instrument of organized taking. He stands upstream of both the Austrian tradition and the public-choice analysis of concentrated benefits and diffuse costs.
