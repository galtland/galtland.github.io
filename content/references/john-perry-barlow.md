# John Perry Barlow

> [[john-perry-barlow|John Perry Barlow]] (1947–2018) was a lyricist for the Grateful Dead, a co-founder of the Electronic Frontier Foundation, and the author of *A Declaration of the Independence of Cyberspace* (1996) — the most quoted single statement of the idea that the networked world lies beyond the reach of the territorial state.

## A Declaration of the Independence of Cyberspace

Barlow wrote the Declaration in February 1996, in Davos, in reaction to the sweeping U.S. Telecommunications Act signed that month. It is addressed, in the second person, to the governments of the world:

> "Governments of the Industrial World, you weary giants of flesh and steel, I come from Cyberspace, the new home of Mind. On behalf of the future, I ask you of the past to leave us alone. You are not welcome among us. You have no sovereignty where we gather."
>
> — [[john-perry-barlow|John Perry Barlow]], *A Declaration of the Independence of Cyberspace*

The argument is a claim about jurisdiction and consent. Cyberspace, Barlow writes, "does not lie within your borders"; it was not built as a public works project and cannot be governed as one. And because governments "derive their just powers from the consent of the governed" — consent the online world never gave — they possess, in his words, "no moral right to rule us nor do you possess any methods of enforcement we have true reason to fear." It is Lockean consent theory turned against the state and pointed at the network.

## The EFF and the Cyber-Libertarian Register

In 1990 Barlow co-founded the Electronic Frontier Foundation with Mitch Kapor and John Gilmore to defend civil liberties online — the organization that would later litigate the [[code-as-speech|code-as-speech]] cases that won First Amendment protection for cryptography. Barlow's role in the movement was rhetorical rather than technical: where [[eric-hughes|Eric Hughes]] told the cypherpunks to *write code* and [[timothy-c-may|Tim May]] supplied the crypto-anarchist analysis, Barlow supplied the anthem. (Barlow's biographical details here are widely documented common knowledge, not drawn from the Declaration this page cites.)

## Sweep and Overreach

The Declaration's power and its weakness are the same sweep. As a statement of the *aspiration* behind [[crypto-anarchy|crypto anarchy]] and the [[the-cybereconomy|cybereconomy]] it is unmatched. As a *prediction* it overreached: states proved entirely willing and largely able to assert jurisdiction over the network — through intermediaries, chokepoints, and the physical infrastructure the "act of nature" actually runs on. The wiki treats the sovereignty claim the way it treats the strong reading of [[jurisdictional-competition|jurisdictional competition]]: technology genuinely raises the cost of control and widens the citizen's exit, without delivering the clean independence the maximalist statement promises. Barlow's document is best read as a charter of intent, not a description of an accomplished fact.

## Place in This Wiki

Barlow marks the exuberant, political edge of the [[cypherpunk|cypherpunk]] thread — the wing that framed encryption and networks as a question of *freedom and sovereignty* rather than only of engineering. His Declaration is the rhetorical bookend to the technical program the wiki traces through [[code-as-speech|code as speech]], and its themes recur, soberer and quantified, in the [[the-cybereconomy|cybereconomy]] and [[seasteading-and-network-states|network-state]] literature.
