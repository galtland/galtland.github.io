# Karl Marx

> Karl Marx (1818–1883) is the German political economist and revolutionary whose [[das-kapital|Capital]] (1867) gave the classical [[subjective-value-vs-the-labor-theory-of-value|labor theory of value]] its most systematic form. For Marx the value of a commodity is an objective magnitude set by the labour embodied in it; this is the position the Austrian subjective theory of value was built to overturn, and which [[eugen-von-bohm-bawerk|Böhm-Bawerk]] set out to refute.

## The Labor Theory of Value

Marx opens *Capital* by analysing the commodity into use-value and value. Setting use-value aside, he argues that what remains common to all commodities, and what their exchange-value expresses, is that they are products of human labour in the abstract: a commodity "has value only because human labour in the abstract has been embodied or materialised in it." The magnitude of that value is then fixed not by individual effort but by social average — "the amount of labour socially necessary, or the labour time socially necessary for its production." Value, on this account, is a property the object *has*, deposited in it by production and measured in labour-time.

From this foundation Marx builds the theory of surplus value: labour-power is itself a commodity whose value is the labour-time needed to reproduce it, while in production it can be made to yield more value than it costs — the surplus appropriated by the capitalist. The objective, production-side determination of value is thus the root of the whole Marxian account of exploitation.

## Place in This Wiki

Marx is the wiki's reference foil for the Austrian theory of value. The [[carl-menger|Mengerian]] revolution of 1871 reverses Marx's order of explanation — value flows from the subjective importance of want-satisfaction to the valuer, not from labour embodied in the object — and [[eugen-von-bohm-bawerk|Böhm-Bawerk]]'s [[karl-marx-and-the-close-of-his-system|Karl Marx and the Close of His System]] presses the internal case that *Capital* Vol. III's competitive "prices of production" cannot be reconciled with Vol. I's labour-values. The head-to-head is drawn out in [[subjective-value-vs-the-labor-theory-of-value|The Subjective Theory of Value vs. the Labor Theory of Value]].
