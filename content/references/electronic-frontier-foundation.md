# Electronic Frontier Foundation

> The [[electronic-frontier-foundation|Electronic Frontier Foundation]] (EFF), founded in 1990, is the leading organization defending civil liberties in the digital world — the legal and advocacy counterpart to the cypherpunks' technical program.

## Founding and mission

The EFF was founded in July 1990 by [[john-perry-barlow|John Perry Barlow]], the software entrepreneur Mitch Kapor, and John Gilmore, in response to what they saw as government overreach against early online communities. Its mission is the defence of free expression, privacy, and innovation online — pursued through litigation, policy advocacy, and technical projects. Where the cypherpunks' answer to threatened privacy was to *write code*, the EFF's was to fight for the legal space in which that code could be written, published, and used. (The EFF's founding details here are widely documented common knowledge, not drawn from the source this page cites.)

## The Crypto Wars

The EFF was a central actor in the 1990s [[pgp-and-the-crypto-wars|Crypto Wars]]. It opposed the **[[key-escrow-and-clipper-chip|Clipper Chip]]** — the government's proposal for an encryption standard with a built-in key-escrow backdoor that would have guaranteed state access to encrypted communications — arguing that mandated backdoors are incompatible with real security and with civil liberties. It fought the encryption **export controls** that classified strong cryptography as a munition and so restricted its publication. The EFF's own archive of the Clipper campaign is one of the primary records of that fight.

## Code as speech

The EFF's most consequential contribution was legal. It backed [[bernstein-v-united-states-1999|Bernstein v. United States]], in which a graduate student challenged the export rules that barred him from publishing his encryption source code. The Ninth Circuit's 1999 ruling that source code is expression protected by the First Amendment — the [[code-as-speech|code-as-speech]] principle — removed the legal ground from under the export-control regime and secured, in law, the freedom to publish cryptography. It is the clearest case of the courtroom winning what the cypherpunks were simultaneously trying to make moot in code.

## Place in This Wiki

The EFF sits at the intersection of the wiki's [[cypherpunk|cypherpunk]] and [[privacy-and-cryptography|privacy-and-cryptography]] threads as their institutional and legal arm. It is a reminder that the cypherpunk victory was not purely technical: the right to deploy strong cryptography was also won in court and in policy, and the EFF is the organization that did most of that winning.
