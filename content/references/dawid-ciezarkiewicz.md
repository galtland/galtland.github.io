# Dawid Ciężarkiewicz

> Dawid Ciężarkiewicz, writing as dpc (@dpc_pw), is cited here for a surviving set of posts on [[libertarianism|libertarianism]]'s coordination problem: how people can cooperate against collective-action failures without creating unaccountable power. The record includes an announced ex-libertarian turn, continuing libertarian-right affinity, and several incompatible-looking remedies. It is evidence of a developing political trajectory, not a systematic treatise or a biography.

## Why the wiki cites him

The posts collected in the three source records make Ciężarkiewicz an unusually direct interlocutor for [[2026-07-22-dpc-coordination-objection|The Coordination Objection]]. He argues that social and economic pressure can constrain people more severely than direct violence; that capitalism maximizes productivity rather than flourishing and can discard productive people as obsolete when more productive rivals appear; and that power can arise in ordinary organizations as well as states. Those documented claims place pressure on libertarian accounts of association, markets, and institutional durability.

## Declared exit and residual allegiance

In September 2022, the documented posts include both an ex-libertarian declaration and a same-day statement of continuing libertarian-right and anarcho-capitalist leanings. Earlier 2022 posts also include support for a coercive governmental role against coordination failures, selective protectionism, and other interventions. These are posts, not a settled doctrinal statement: they establish neither a clean break from libertarianism nor a stable alternative.

## Three objections

His first objection concerns the perimeter of liberty. A society can impose severe social and economic costs through consensus, exclusion, and refusal to deal even where no one initiates physical force. His second treats [[capitalism|capitalism]] as a cybernetic selection process and challenges the claim that its outcomes track leisure, dignity, or well-being. His third holds that power remains possible in any arrangement and can consolidate in clubs, technical projects, and societies.

The thesis's replies and distinctions are interpretations of these posts, not claims made by Ciężarkiewicz himself. In particular, it distinguishes the potential for power from a guarantee of consolidation and asks which institutional designs affect power's scale, scope, contestability, and returns.

## Competing coordination remedies

The record does not point in one direction. A 2022 post assigns government a coercive role in preventing coordination failures. A 2024 post instead proposes a religion-like group with binding in-group rules, invoking halal or kosher food and [[nassim-taleb|Taleb]]'s [[minority-rule|minority rule]]. Other posts describe competition among jurisdictions as a restraint on a global political monopoly, treat [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]]'s consensus rules as a coordination device, or commend higher-level economic optimization alongside bottom-level markets.

These remedies raise different questions about coercion, exit, rule change, knowledge, and capture. The surviving posts do not resolve their relationship to one another, so this page does not treat any one as his final position.

## Scope and limits

The three source records are an ideological extract, recovered 2022 posts, and a recovered 2024–2026 supplement; they are not a complete archive of an account. Some posts are deleted or only partly recoverable, and political remarks form a small portion of a feed otherwise concerned with engineering. Absence from the archive is therefore weak evidence. The visible sequence is also non-monotonic: later posts can qualify, sit alongside, or leave unresolved earlier ones rather than supersede them.
