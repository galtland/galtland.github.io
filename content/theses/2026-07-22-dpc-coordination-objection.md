# The Coordination Objection: A Reply to an Ex-Libertarian

> The most instructive critique of [[libertarianism|libertarianism]] in this corpus comes not from a hostile outsider but from a developer who knows the arguments, builds the tools, and left anyway. [[dawid-ciezarkiewicz|dpc]]'s three objections — that society binds harder than the state, that [[capitalism|capitalism]] optimizes productivity rather than flourishing, and that power can re-consolidate — land partly on ground this corpus already concedes in its own voice. Traced across four years, his trajectory is not a clean exit from the tradition, but neither is it wholly inside it: it moves between libertarian constraints and coercive coordination remedies. His May 2024 remedy for Moloch — a voluntary group whose binding in-group rules work on the halal/kosher model — resembles the unifying meaning that the companion adoption-problem thesis describes as still unbuilt; his coordination-kernel government reads as [[minarchism|minarchism]] only at its narrowest, his 2022 posts giving it a wider, coercive brief. His June 2026 turn toward higher-level holistic economic optimization poses a distinct comparative-institutions question: if higher-level optimization entails economy-wide coercive authority able to override allocation, what makes that concentration sufficiently bounded, contestable, and resistant to capture? His irreducible-power premise does not answer that question, and no surviving post in the cited corpus supplies the institutional comparison.

## The interlocutor

Dawid Ciężarkiewicz, who writes as dpc (@dpc_pw), is a working Rust developer in [[bitcoin|Bitcoin]] and Fedimint — federated [[ecash|ecash]] — infrastructure. He knows the arguments and left anyway, so his departure cannot be answered by introducing him to arguments he already holds: his objection is not that the tradition lacks an account of property, prices, or aggression, but that those accounts do not solve the coordination problems that actually bind individuals. In September 2022 he declared the exit (the spelling in all quotations here is his own):

> I'm an ex-libertarian. I eventually understood and accepted some things: 1) Social and economical pressures are far, far more dangerous and limiting to individual freedom than direct "violence". Humans are social animals and can't survive on their own.
>
> — [[dawid-ciezarkiewicz|Dawid Ciężarkiewicz]] (@dpc_pw), X post, 12 Sep 2022

The same thread continues:

> Group will always impose its will overpowering the individual. Government is just a formalization of that. You maybe can get rid of government, but you can't get rid of the group. 4) Markets and individual choice are far more stupid and hopeless than what libertarians believe.
>
> — [[dawid-ciezarkiewicz|Dawid Ciężarkiewicz]] (@dpc_pw), X post, 12 Sep 2022

And, the same day, a self-description:

> Note: I was (and am still strongly leaning) libertarian right ancap.
>
> — [[dawid-ciezarkiewicz|Dawid Ciężarkiewicz]] (@dpc_pw), X post, 12 Sep 2022

The label coexisted with heterodox positions already in the record: in the months before this post he had endorsed selective protectionism, assigned government a coercive role against coordination failures, once named an unspecified income-inequality intervention among a country's needs, and floated banning or taxing usury. Because those posts predate the self-description, they complicate the label rather than retract it — the weight placed on his stated continuing ancap lean is bounded to an affinity, not a doctrine. No later surviving post in the cited corpus retracts it.

His political posts are a small fraction of a feed otherwise given to Rust, Nix, and Bitcoin engineering, and two limits follow from that. The record is incomplete, since some of what he wrote has been deleted and cannot be read, so the absence of a view is weak evidence. And the posts that remain are the asides of a working engineer, evidence of a trajectory rather than a worked-out program. This reply reads them as a first-person account of exit from anarcho-capitalism by someone fluent in the canon — which is what makes it a rare test case — and holds its conclusions with the modesty owed to a position reconstructed from asides.

## What should be conceded first

Nothing below is argued from a fortress. [[objections-to-libertarianism|Objections to Libertarianism]] names the empirical adoption and stability of a free society among the points where the corpus's answers are weakest. [[criticisms-of-anarcho-capitalism|Criticisms of Anarcho-Capitalism]] rates its own confidence medium, holds [[robert-nozick|Nozick]]'s invisible-hand argument — that a dominant protective agency, and with it a minimal state, would legitimately re-emerge from anarchy — genuinely unresolved, and concedes that no institutional design is self-enforcing: durability rests on culture and ideas more than on formal structure. So when dpc presses on stability, consolidation, and adoption, he is pressing where the tradition, in its honest moments, already gives way. The task of a reply is to establish what his objections do and do not license.

## First claim: society binds harder than the state

*Stated at full strength: a society can respect every formal property title while coordinating so thoroughly against a minority that the minority loses meaningful social and economic agency. If libertarianism calls that society free because no one initiates violence, the [[nonaggression-and-property-rights|non-aggression principle]] guards the wrong perimeter.*

His test case, put to libertarians as a direct question:

> Is a cast system in which super-majority of population forms a socially enforced consensus on treating an arbitrary minority like shit, denying them social and economic opportunities OK with libertarians, as long as NAP is not vilated?
>
> — [[dawid-ciezarkiewicz|Dawid Ciężarkiewicz]] (@dpc_pw), X post, 12 Sep 2022

The question is built as a reductio, and the honest answer is to bite it. Under [[nonaggression-and-property-rights|nonaggression and property rights]], yes: a caste order enforced purely through shunning, refusal to deal, and social consensus violates no one's rights. The canon's most systematic treatment of exactly this terrain — [[hans-hermann-hoppe|Hoppe]]'s account of [[forced-integration|forced integration]] in [[democracy-the-god-that-failed|Democracy: The God That Failed]] — does not deny the possibility. It treats state-mandated association as the aggression, answers it with voluntary separation, and accepts the illiberal consequence without flinching: covenant communities with entrance requirements, exclusion on whatever criteria their members set. A bullet bitten, not dodged.

Where the objection is strongest is in what the rule measures. [[hannah-arendt|Arendt]]'s distinction in [[on-violence|On Violence]] between [[violence-vs-power|violence and power]] substantially concedes his premise from outside the tradition: power is what people have when they act in concert, and a supermajority's capacity to ruin someone without laying a finger on him is power in the primary sense — the state one formalization of it, which is very close to his own sentence about government. A rule indexed to violence is indexed to the derivative variable, exactly as he says. The concession is about scope, not truth — a rule about when force may be used was never a theory of everything that can go wrong in a human life — but it does make the answer that the NAP is not violated a jurisdictional reply rather than a moral one, and he is right to find it thin.

What the concession does not establish is that a higher sovereign is the cure, and his own words close the escape route: "Government is just a formalization of that." Grant it: then a state empowered to break the caste is the formalization of the very supermajority whose "socially enforced consensus" constitutes it — the group's instrument, not a neutral third party, and so no automatic cure. The NAP guards the violence perimeter not because its theorists judged social pressure harmless, but because that is the one line where the guard does not become the thing guarded against. His anthropological premise, meanwhile, is the tradition's own starting point rather than a discovery deployed against it: [[samuel-pufendorf|Pufendorf]] derives the whole interpersonal law of nature from [[sociality|sociality]] — man too needy to survive without his own kind — and distinguishes imperfect duties of courtesy, whose neglect may be rude or cruel but is not ordinarily compellable, from strict rights. That framework can condemn ordinary refusals without licensing force; it is not a categorical answer to dpc's caste case, because Pufendorf's own treatment of extreme want makes otherwise noncompellable aid claimable when necessary for preservation. Pufendorf himself retains a civil sovereign, so the parallel is to his derivation, not his politics; the real disagreement is the direction of the deduction, from the person's dependence outward or from the group's standing authority down, and the shared premise settles it for neither side.

What remains is the practical question of what erodes a caste consensus, and the tradition's answer is narrower than he wants but not empty: competition, persuasion, exit, the standing cost [[milton-friedman|Friedman]] identifies in [[capitalism-and-freedom|Capitalism and Freedom]] — the man who discriminates pays for it, while rivals have an incentive to hire or serve people he refuses — and the mechanism dpc himself later proposes: intolerant minorities can organize against a caste consensus as well as for one. Whether these instruments are sufficient against a determined supermajority is an empirical question the corpus does not settle. The reply is narrower than a victory: the non-aggression principle remains indispensable as a rule governing enforceable claims and insufficient as a complete theory of social freedom. He is entitled to find that unsatisfying; the question of who enforces the better answer remains either way.

## Second claim: the wrong objective function

*Stated at full strength: market competition is not neutral coordination but a selection process — a cybernetic system that rewards whatever captures revenue and survives, productivity rather than leisure, dignity, or flourishing.*

His earliest formulation predates the exit:

> There's no escaping the fact that capitalism is just a cybernetic system that maximizes productivity growth. Not "humanness", free time, well being or anything else. Only productivity. And productivity is often highly correlated with human well-being. But especially in recent …
>
> — [[dawid-ciezarkiewicz|Dawid Ciężarkiewicz]] (@dpc_pw), X post, 30 Jul 2022

A continuation of the same thread sharpens what he means by "maximizes":

> And no this is not based on the idea of limited resources. You might be perfectly productive today, but someone more productive shows up tomorrow, and capitalism throws you away as obsolete, not worth maintenance cost anymore. …
>
> — [[dawid-ciezarkiewicz|Dawid Ciężarkiewicz]] (@dpc_pw), X post, 30 Jul 2022

By 2026 it had compressed into aphorism — "Capitalism is a good servant, but bad master." — and the sharper charge that "capitalism is about the value you can capture, and not the value you create."

Concede the capture point first, because the tradition already makes it at length. The corpus's name for returns that flow to political position rather than production is [[crony-capitalism|crony capitalism]]: [[frederic-bastiat|Bastiat]]'s legal plunder, [[murray-n-rothbard|Rothbard]]'s grants of monopoly privilege, and the [[public-choice-and-rational-ignorance|public-choice]] logic that makes rent-seeking a standing tendency rather than an occasional abuse. The libertarian move is to call this the [[political-means-and-economic-means|political means]] wearing a market mask and therefore not [[capitalism|capitalism]]; he would reply that a distinction unenforceable in practice is not much of a distinction — and a distinction the corpus itself treats as contested. Applied to the Western hybrid that actually exists — the system his February 2026 post describes as "Government overtaken by capital" — his capture line is not an objection to the tradition's account. It is the tradition's account.

The optimizer frame is where the disagreement actually lives. An emergent cybernetic selection process need not contain, or represent anywhere, a global objective function. [[friedrich-a-hayek|Hayek]]'s [[spontaneous-order|spontaneous order]] and the [[knowledge-problem|knowledge problem]] describe coordination among differing ends, not a system consciously optimizing one end. The surviving first post breaks off at "But especially in recent"; its correlation sentence therefore cannot be treated as the last word of the qualification. A later recovered post in the thread sharpens the complaint that can be assessed: firms seek profit and survival, and competition can reward relative productivity while making an otherwise productive worker or firm obsolete. Productivity is one route to revenue and survival, not the sole output selected. Consumer choices help determine what is rewarded; worker choices do too, including choices of leisure that are often made by foregoing income. That does not make markets neutral among all human goods: non-priced values and externalities can be neglected, and exit can be constrained by wealth, dependence, or social power. The categorical only-productivity charge is therefore too strong, but the complaint about outcomes people reasonably reject is not.

The appropriate response need not be either laissez-faire acquiescence or comprehensive planning. Property, liability, associational rules, and other polycentric rule changes can alter incentives and remedy particular harms, though each requires its own account of authority, knowledge, and capture. A higher-level program also faces an aggregation problem when it claims to rank plural ends, but that problem does not show that every correction must abolish markets. By July 2024 the complaint is fully formed and the remedy left explicitly blank: humanity should "organize using tools of cybernetics, distributed systems, complexity theory, game theory, etc. serving our philosophical beliefs and goals." He adds, "Yet there's nothing remotely like that on a political horizon anywhere." In the same post, liberal capitalism was still "the best we can do". His June 2026 post supplies one possible direction, but the comparative question remains which institutions can correct specific failures without creating worse knowledge or capture problems.

## Third claim: power always comes back

*Stated at full strength: every arrangement contains decisions about rules, enforcement, admission, and change. Those decisions create power, and organization can concentrate it. No design abolishes the potential for power or guarantees against consolidation.*

The mature statement came in July 2026:

> "Real anarcho-capitalism have never been tried."
>
> The libertarians have instincts right, but refuse to accept/realize that potential of power is just there, irreducible, and someone sooner or later is going to consolidate it.
>
> Barely ever can have HoA or a chess club without power drama, and somehow we are supposed to have whole societies...
>
> — [[dawid-ciezarkiewicz|Dawid Ciężarkiewicz]] (@dpc_pw), X post, 15 Jul 2026

It had been building since 2023:

> In practice 99% of the public has no idea what's going on and delegates power to a combination of aristocrats (developers) and oligarchs (businesses). Bitcoin is not exempt from the Iron Law of Oligarchy.
>
> — [[dawid-ciezarkiewicz|Dawid Ciężarkiewicz]] (@dpc_pw), X post, 30 Jan 2023

This is the objection that lands on the corpus's weakest ground, and the concessions above already grant most of it: his HoA-and-chess-club line is a compressed statement of the stability objection the corpus itself takes most seriously. It is the strongest of his three claims, and it is conceded.

The relevant qualification is that the prospects for consolidation are not fixed across designs. [[megapolitics|Megapolitics]] — the frame of [[the-sovereign-individual|The Sovereign Individual]] — and the [[offense-defense-balance-of-technology|offense–defense balance of technology]] treat the returns to consolidation as a technological variable rather than a constant: when technology creates increasing returns to large-scale violence, coercive organizations tend to grow; when small groups gain cost-effective defensive capacity or returns to large-scale violence fall, power can fragment. That does not repeal the [[iron-law-of-oligarchy|Iron Law of Oligarchy]] or provide a guarantee. It identifies offense–defense and protocol design as relevant variables. The wide historical variation in consolidation is evidence for asking those institutional questions rather than treating all designs as equivalent. This is a wager, not a law. His Bitcoin example gives the institutional comparison a concrete case:

> Personal choice doesn't work collectively. [...] I am freedom loving, but at some point one needs to realize that voluntarily* restricting one's collective short-term freedom is beneficial for one's individual freedom in the long-term.
>
> Bitcoin works precisely because all its users agree to follow restrictive consensus rules. [...] World is full of coordination traps. Unless humans realize they need to navigate these balancing short term and long term, individual and collective, freedom and responsibility, we're doomed.
>
> — [[dawid-ciezarkiewicz|Dawid Ciężarkiewicz]] (@dpc_pw), X post, 26 Sep 2024

The important word is *voluntarily*. Bitcoin does not eliminate protocol power; it makes rules explicit, verification broadly available, and continued participation conditional on acceptance. As [[21-lessons|21 Lessons]] emphasizes, changing consensus rules requires convincing users to adopt them. [[self-custody|Self-Custody]] raises seizure costs, while [[proof-of-work|proof of work]] raises block-production and history-rewrite costs. His Iron Law post says Bitcoin is not exempt — granted, and this corpus claims exemption for no one. But *not exempt* and *unaffected* are different claims: the design did not abolish the gradient; it changed some costs of climbing it. His own Bitcoin example therefore treats institutional design as consequential. No design abolishes the potential for power or guarantees against consolidation; offense–defense and protocol design can nevertheless affect its scale, scope, contestability, and returns. That is weaker than any promise to eliminate rule and stronger than treating institutional design as irrelevant.

## The convergence: his remedy has the shape of the tradition's open problem

The post on which the convergence rests is from May 2024 — but it was not his first answer to Moloch. In July 2022 the same problem had the opposite solution:

> There is an obvious role for governments and it's keeping Moloch at bay (preventing negative social outcomes of Prisoner's Dilemma). No, free markets can't do it, because it requires coercion. Spare me your libertarian tears.
>
> — [[dawid-ciezarkiewicz|Dawid Ciężarkiewicz]] (@dpc_pw), X post, 12 Jul 2022

That answer is governmental and explicitly coercive, markets declared incapable and the libertarian objection dismissed in advance. The May 2024 post, in the narrower context of freedom-preserving software, reaches instead for a voluntary group:

> The only solution to Moloh is "religious fundamentalism" like coordination. The reason why software keeps enslaving is that Freedom preserving software can't compete (mostly economically) with larger economy of slave software.
>
> If we were able to create religion like group that one can join to gain benefits, but must respect in group rules, we could win. Think "halal" or "cosher" food, religious fundamentalism. Google Taleb's "the most intolerant win".
>
> — [[dawid-ciezarkiewicz|Dawid Ciężarkiewicz]] (@dpc_pw), X post, 10 May 2024

The remedy for Moloch — his shorthand for the coordination trap in which freedom-preserving options lose to better-funded unfree ones — is a group joined voluntarily for its benefits, whose in-group rules genuinely bind, propagating on the [[minority-rule|minority rule]] that [[nassim-taleb|Taleb]] names: a compatible product or standard can spread because a strict minority accepts only one option while a flexible majority accepts either, and separate provisioning costs more than offering the common option. That is a design, not a mood. Between the two posts, on his own signature problem, the remedy moved from state coercion to voluntary binding rules — and the later one runs toward the tradition's frontier, not away from it. He never says which it is: whether the 2024 proposal superseded the 2022 governmental remedy, supplemented it, or answered a narrower question about software. No later surviving post in the cited corpus retracts the 2022 position, and the 2024 "only solution" formulation displaces it without acknowledgment — a limit that travels with the claim.

Now set beside it the argument in [[2026-06-08-libertarianism-and-the-adoption-problem|Libertarianism and Human Nature: The Adoption Problem]]: the classic routes to a freer order are each necessary and none sufficient; what mobilizes people is belonging rather than argument; and the missing piece is a still-unbuilt unifying meaning capable of supplying the belonging [[nationalism-and-state-formation|nationalism]] supplies. These are the same shape at different resolutions — his describes the institutional rules, the thesis the belonging that makes people willing to follow them — and his is the more operational: benefits at the door, binding rules inside, an intolerant minority setting the standard. It even has a precedent in the tradition he left: Hoppe's covenant communities, territorial and proprietary where his model is non-territorial observance.

Note the structural symmetry with his own caste case, because it is load-bearing: the halal/kosher device is a socially enforced consensus that binds individuals without violating property — precisely the mechanism his 2022 question presented as libertarianism's embarrassment. The two are not the same object — they differ in purpose, in heredity, in what happens to those outside, and voluntary entry distinguishes the 2024 proposal from a hereditary caste — so the convergence is the narrower, structural point: his proposed group operates in the same institutional space where the tradition locates voluntary association and exclusion, the space [[secession|secession]] and [[seasteading-and-network-states|network states]] occupy in this corpus. The post leaves exit and enforcement unspecified; on the reading that keeps them within voluntary exit and the owner's right to exclude, such in-group rules are associational conditions rather than binding contracts — and under the [[title-transfer-theory-of-contract|title-transfer theory of contract]], which enforces transferred title rather than a bare promise, that is the ordinary right to exclude the [[market-anarchism-and-private-law|private-law order]] is made of. Whether dpc's own group would stay within those bounds, the post does not say. What the post supplies is permission and a sketch, not evidence of an existing institution: it names no group already implementing the design.

His other recurring formulations converge the same way. One puts the coordination function directly:

> What libertarians don't get is that there is an obvious role for "the government", and it's the same as for OS kernel - coordination. The problem with smoking in public places was the "rule of intolerant minority". …
>
> — [[dawid-ciezarkiewicz|Dawid Ciężarkiewicz]] (@dpc_pw), X post, 11 Sep 2022

A minimal layer legitimated by function resembles [[minarchism|minarchism]] — the night-watchman state argued from systems architecture instead of natural rights. But the metaphor fixes a coordination *function*, not a night-watchman *scope*, and his 2022 posts show he had already filled that scope in with something larger. Two months before the kernel post, the same "obvious role for governments" formula named the role coercively: "keeping Moloch at bay (preventing negative social outcomes of Prisoner's Dilemma)", with markets declared unable to do it "because it requires coercion"; and the same spring he praised the "intelligent use of selective protectionism" of key industries. Minarchism is what the metaphor licenses at minimum, not what he meant — which is why the June 2026 turn reasserts a scope he had shown before rather than inventing one. One month after the exit declaration, he had also already stated the tradition's other answer to consolidation from scratch:

> If you are a Bitcoiner you need to understand: we're all trapped by game theory: we have to support a terrible lying violent gang to protect us from other violent gangs. And having one global gang would be even bigger disaster because it would eliminate the competition
>
> — [[dawid-ciezarkiewicz|Dawid Ciężarkiewicz]] (@dpc_pw), X post, 16 Oct 2022

That is [[jurisdictional-competition|jurisdictional competition]] in its harshest vocabulary: the answer to irreducible power is not its abolition but its division, so that exit disciplines the price. He will need that argument again below. And his cultural conclusion at the end of 2024 — the ideal human civilization as space-faring Amish, keeping selected technology while refusing what he saw as modernity's self-destructive defaults — is a technologically selective, norm-governed cultural image. It does not by itself establish terms of voluntary entry or exit.

None of this denies his chosen label. Several of his remedies — voluntary rule-bound association, competitive jurisdictions, protocol constraints, a common coordination layer — sit inside the tradition's unresolved institutional frontier; his 2022 posts show others that do not. On the voluntarist half of his thinking he is working on a problem this corpus does not identify the tradition as having solved.

## The holistic optimizer: a comparative-institutions challenge

In June 2026 he supplies a candidate for the higher-level corrector his objective-function critique had implied:

> In The Goal, it's well explained how the cardinal sin of system optimization is optimizing each part in separation. Capitalism is basically each part of the economy optimizing not only in separation but adversarialy to the whole...
>
> In Thermoeconomics in Time of Monsters, it's well explained how Chinese economic model is a combination of bottom level capitalism with higher-level holistic economic system optimization, all contrasted with western economic model.
>
> Quite clear to me that it's a superior way to think about economics, where western squables of naive capitalism with naive socialism now seem like backwater superstitions.
>
> — [[dawid-ciezarkiewicz|Dawid Ciężarkiewicz]] (@dpc_pw), X post, 26 Jun 2026

The systems insight is sound at the level of an engineered organization: optimizing each component's local metric can degrade a designed whole. The inference to an economy does not follow automatically, because a factory has an owner, a defined output, and a purpose supplied from outside the system, while a society contains people with different purposes and no uncontested scalar measure of the whole.

The Austrian objection has to be scoped honestly. [[ludwig-von-mises|Mises]]'s [[economic-calculation-problem|calculation problem]] is an in-principle result about full socialization: when private ownership, exchange, and money prices for capital goods are abolished, rational economic calculation is impossible rather than merely hard. A market-retaining hybrid keeps capital-goods prices and therefore does not trigger that full impossibility result. It may still distort prices through interventions, and it still faces the knowledge and capture problems; neither point defines the hybrid as not holistic by fiat. Its institutional character depends on what its higher-level authority may direct, override, or leave to market participants. The knowledge problem remains relevant wherever officials substitute their judgments for local knowledge that prices summarize, while any claim to optimize a whole must explain how it ranks plural ends. Neither argument proves anything about a particular economy's growth statistics; both identify trade-offs that an interventionist hybrid must answer.

The damaging objection, though, is not Austrian. It is his own, and he supplies it himself four months earlier:

> As a very pro free markets and personal freedom loving person, I think it is a good time to take an L and acknowledge that just like communism in practice degenerates into totalitarian dystopia, free markets / capitalism degenerates into ... this ... what the West has right now. Government overtaken by capital, masses hypnotized by propaganda, secret societies worshiping Moloch, military industrial complex killing millions, social fabric dismantled.
>
> You might laugh at "real communism have never been tried", and then look at all that is happening and say... "acktually, this is not a real capitalism"? Basically "real capitalism have never been tried"?
>
> — [[dawid-ciezarkiewicz|Dawid Ciężarkiewicz]] (@dpc_pw), X post, 11 Feb 2026

"Government overtaken by capital" is his capture diagnosis of the Western hybrid he criticizes. His endorsement of the Chinese model names a superior way to think about economics rather than specifying a determinate institution. If higher-level optimization entails economy-wide coercive authority able to override allocation, however, it creates a broad point at which firms may seek political advantage rather than create value. His irreducible-power premise does not demonstrate that such capture will occur or make the endorsement self-contradictory. It makes the comparison of institutions unavoidable: what limits the authority's scope, who can contest its decisions, what countervailing centers remain, and why should its expected capture costs be lower than those of the arrangements it replaces? The cited posts do not answer those questions.

His February post also supplies the discipline that should govern the comparison: he refuses to let capitalism be judged by its brochure — "real capitalism have never been tried" is, on his telling, a laugh line of the same species as its communist twin, and by July 2026 he extends the same mockery to "Real anarcho-capitalism have never been tried." Consistency asks that a proposed higher-level arrangement face the same test. Its case cannot rest on a benevolent description alone; it needs an account of rule change, authority, contestation, and the comparative risk of capture. An April 2024 post identifies the issue directly:

> The question of power still remains: someone has to get to change (or not) the rules.
>
> — [[dawid-ciezarkiewicz|Dawid Ciężarkiewicz]] (@dpc_pw), X post, 11 Apr 2024

If higher-level optimization entails economy-wide coercive authority able to override allocation, that question is especially acute, not decisively settled. No later surviving post in the cited corpus supplies the institutional account. The tension remains an unanswered comparative-institutions challenge.

## The record is not monotonic

A fair reading notes the posts that cut the other way, because the drift is not a straight line. As late as September 2023, a year after the exit, he was still defending capitalism against its lazier critics — the people who never complain about it, he observed, are those who experienced its lack — and in July 2024 he still judged liberal capitalism, for all its flaws, "the best we can do". The competing-gangs post, as libertarian an argument as he has ever made, came one month after the exit declaration; and the 2022 record holds both strands at once — he could write "Sure ideally taxes would be 0%" in the same August 2022 in which he was already weighing whether to "Ban usury altogether" or "Tax usury", weeks after the coercive anti-Moloch post, and two years before the November 2024 post that rejects the libertarian objection directly — its self-description pragmatist rather than convert, its ground basic dignity rather than a rival system, and nothing general about markets following from it.

An August 2025 post puts a third instrument on the same problem the 2024 remedy addressed. Against the prospect of Google and Apple removing Bitcoin apps, and with sideloading noted as itself revocable, he concludes that "the only real and lasting solution is political" — the remedy named being a constitutional amendment guaranteeing freedom of computing. On its visible text that is a limit on power rather than a mandate to coordinate, which would place the remedy inside the tradition rather than outside it. But only the opening of the post survives in readable form, cut off just as it turns to the companies themselves, and an amendment binding private platforms would be a mandate on private property of the kind the tradition refuses — so neither how far he carried the thought nor which way it finally cuts can be read. What is legible is a third answer to the problem, offered a year after the voluntary one.

The pattern across four years is someone holding a market prior and a coordination prior at the same time. That is not confusion but an accurate report of the state of the problem: the 2026 post makes the comparative institutional question more concrete without resolving it.

## What remains contested

Nothing here settles the disagreement, and several pieces of it cannot be settled from this corpus at all.

- **Whether the non-coercive remedies are sufficient.** That competition, persuasion, and exit erode a caste order is a prediction, not a demonstration; voluntary separation shows a NAP-consistent answer exists, not that a society of exclusive covenants would be good to live in. The gap between a rights-answer and a flourishing-answer is the gap his objection names.
- **Whether the state comes back anyway.** Nozick's re-emergence argument is unresolved on its merits; if the stability concession is fatal, it is fatal to minarchism and the holistic optimizer as well — but it is fatal to anarcho-capitalism first.
- **Whether the cost asymmetry holds.** The defense-scaling claim is an admittedly uncertain wager; the [[parallel-economy|parallel-economy]] strategy and [[2026-07-02-bitcoin-frees-the-individual-not-the-collective|Bitcoin Frees the Individual, Not the Collective]] rest on it and are honest that it is a wager.
- **Whether value capture is corrigible.** The standing objection is that the distinction between market profit and political favor is unenforceable in practice; the corpus does not resolve this, and his version of the complaint is the strong one.
- **Whether a voluntary creed can bind without curdling.** A group strong enough to discipline defectors needs a theory of dissent, expulsion, rule change, and exit costs; without one, religion-like coordination can become a socially enforced caste in another form. His halal/kosher proposal and the adoption thesis's creed-and-coordination problem are one question: the cited post names no existing group that answers it, while the companion thesis presents the unifying meaning as unfinished work.

What can be said plainly is narrower. His first and third claims are conceded here in the terms he set them. The second is conceded in its rent-seeking half and answered in its cybernetic half: an emergent selection process need not have a represented global objective, and productivity is not its only route or output. It is not, though, his only departure from libertarian policy: his 2022 posts also hold positions outside any strict anarcho-capitalist or minarchist frontier, coercive government against coordination failures and selective protectionism among them. His trajectory is therefore neither a clean exit nor an orbit wholly inside the tradition; it moves between libertarian constraints and coercive coordination remedies, and the honest description is that motion, not a single fixed position. The frontier the voluntarist half of his thinking works is not whether people need coordination — they do. It is whether coordination can become binding enough to defeat collective-action traps while remaining plural, contestable, and cheap to leave. The sources in this corpus do not show that it can. If higher-level optimization entails economy-wide coercive authority able to override allocation, its advocates must make the comparative institutional case for its limits, knowledge, contestability, and capture resistance; no later surviving post in the cited corpus supplies that case. Whatever he calls himself, on the voluntarist side of his thinking he is working on a problem this corpus does not identify the tradition as having solved.
