# The April 2026 FOMC Rate Hold: ABCT and the Knowledge Problem

> URL: https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/monetary20260429a.htm
>
> The Federal Open Market Committee held the federal funds target range at 3-1/2 to 3-3/4 percent at its April 29, 2026 meeting. The statement said economic activity has been expanding at a solid pace, job gains have remained low, and inflation is elevated, in part reflecting a recent increase in global energy prices. The decision drew four dissents: Stephen Miran preferred a 1/4-point cut, while Beth Hammack, Neel Kashkari, and Lorie Logan supported holding but opposed the statement's easing bias. Eight members, including Chair Powell and Vice Chair Williams, voted in favor.
>
> — News post, 2026-06-03

A target range for overnight bank reserves is an administered price for marginal bank credit. [[state-power-and-intervention|State Power and Intervention]] identifies the institutional move: the loan market is not left to clear through saver and borrower bids. [[knowledge-problem|Knowledge Problem]] explains why four dissents are not incidental personnel drama; the relevant savings, risk, and project information is dispersed and changing. If the chosen rate sits below the level voluntary saving would set, [[mises-on-credit-expansion|Mises on Credit Expansion]] gives the immediate effect: business calculation is falsified. [[austrian-business-cycle-theory|Austrian Business Cycle Theory]] gives the consequence: longer projects are funded as if the real saved resources existed, and [[rothbard-on-fed-induced-boom|Rothbard on Fed-Induced Booms]] names the resulting cluster of errors. The headline choice - hold, cut, or easing bias - is not the cycle signal. It is the administrative vocabulary of an intervention whose evidence must be looked for in fiduciary-media issuance, sectoral skew, and later liquidation.

## The Rate Is a Price

The federal funds target is not a neutral observation of credit conditions. [[credit-expansion-dynamics|Credit Expansion Dynamics]] treats a bank of issue's interest-rate setting as its credit policy: new fiduciary media are placed by lending below the natural rate, drawing in borrowers who would not have borrowed at the rate set by voluntary saving. Administering that benchmark through a monetary monopoly also makes the policy a triangular intervention in Rothbard's sense: the state imposes terms on exchange between other parties, here savers, borrowers, and banks ([[intervention-taxonomy|Rothbard's Taxonomy of Intervention]]).

The benchmark is the natural rate - the rate at which time preference and voluntary saving would clear the loan market. [[the-theory-of-money-and-credit|The Theory of Money and Credit]] states the constraint directly:

> If they demand less than the natural rate of interest - and they must do this if they wish to do any business at all with the new issue of fiduciary media; it must not be forgotten that they are offering an additional supply of credit to the market - then these requests will increase.

The natural rate is not posted for the committee to inspect. It is the outcome of market exchange. Without the exchange that discovers the price, there is no figure for the planner to read; that is the monetary form of the [[economic-calculation-problem|Economic Calculation Problem]]. The split vote is therefore intelligible: identical aggregate data, different administrative estimates, no market price to settle the matter.

## The Vote Is Not the Cycle Signal

A hold does not say whether policy is tight, neutral, or still expansionary. [[monetary-aggregates-and-credit-expansion|Monetary Aggregates and Credit Expansion]] separates the policy input from the cycle signal: a policy rate affects banks' cost of marginal liquidity, but it does not measure the quantity or composition of credit expansion that follows. The theoretical test - whether the gross market rate is suppressed below the natural rate - cannot be verified directly.

The gross-market-rate mechanism is the load-bearing step. In [[human-action|Human Action]], the Mises passage quoted in [[mises-on-credit-expansion|Mises on Credit Expansion]] gives the calculational effect:

> But now the drop in interest rates falsifies the businessman's calculation. Although the amount of capital goods available did not increase, the calculation employs figures which would be utilizable only if such an increase had taken place. The result of such calculations is therefore misleading.

Rothbard's institutional version in [[americas-great-depression|America's Great Depression]], quoted in [[rothbard-on-fed-induced-boom|Rothbard on Fed-Induced Booms]], supplies the central-bank application:

> In the purely free and unhampered market, there will be no cluster of errors, since trained entrepreneurs will not all make errors at the same time. The "boom-bust" cycle is generated by monetary intervention in the market, specifically bank credit expansion to business.

That is the context behind the phrase "opposed the statement's easing bias." The dissenters resisted the direction of the statement, not the architecture of rate administration. A cut can deepen a distortion; a hold can preserve a prior one; hawkish language can concede the inflation symptom while leaving the rate-setting mechanism intact. The relevant evidence is not the committee verb. It is money and credit growth, bank lending, interest-rate-sensitive sectors, and the production structure.

## Inflation Is a Money Relation

The statement says inflation is "elevated, in part reflecting a recent increase in global energy prices." A rise in energy prices is a relative-price change. It redirects spending and changes measured indexes; by itself it does not explain a sustained fall in money's purchasing power. [[cash-holding-and-the-demand-for-money|Cash Holding and the Demand for Money]] gives the relevant frame: purchasing power is determined by the money relation, the supply of money against the demand to hold it.

That distinction matters because the issuer of base money is describing the symptom. An energy shock can raise particular prices and can move an index. A general and sustained elevation of prices requires the monetary side: money proper, fiduciary media, and the demand to hold them. Treating the symptom as chiefly an input-price event points away from the relation the central bank itself most directly alters.

## Scope

This is a context case, not a dated bust call. The post does not measure the natural-rate gap, the path of fiduciary media, bank-credit behavior, or the sectoral pattern of investment. A rate hold is not itself credit expansion. The cycle claim requires net new fiduciary-media issuance and the structural evidence that follows from it. The narrower claim is enough: a committee administering the price of credit is attempting to replace market discovery with an estimate, and the neutral policy register hides that the estimate is itself the intervention.
